How it works

Transmit. Text becomes bytes. A length byte goes in front and a CRC-8 checksum goes on the end. With Hamming on, every 4 bits becomes 7 bits. Each bit is then played as a tone — one frequency for 0, another for 1. A steady lead tone plays first so the receiver knows where the message starts.

Receive. The mic stream is chopped into slices. Each slice is correlated against both tone frequencies using the Goertzel algorithm, which is a single-frequency DFT — a matched filter for one tone. Whichever frequency has more energy wins, and that gives the bit. Hamming repairs any single flipped bit per group of 7; CRC then confirms the whole message survived.

Why FSK and not amplitude. Phones apply automatic gain control to the mic, so absolute loudness is unreliable. Deciding on which of two frequencies is louder is immune to that.
